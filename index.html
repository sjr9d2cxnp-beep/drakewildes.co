Customer Reality

In waste, beverage, and vocational fleets the driver is still the main signal. When a truck goes down, teams spend time verifying what happened and rebuilding the picture across systems. The data is there. The hard part is turning it into a decision before the window closes.

How I Run Discovery

The same structure I use to evaluate incomplete signals on high-consequence equipment maps to the discovery sequence in a sales conversation. The SE qualifies fit and shapes the next step. The customer owns the operational decision.

1. Detect the Real Problem A reported fault is an indicator, not a conclusion. Separate what the system is showing from what people assume.
2. Validate With Evidence Confirm the constraint before recommending a next step. Only advance when the data supports it.
3. Right-Size the Next Step Use the smallest action that moves the evaluation or the deal forward.
4. Quantify Operational Impact Frame the discussion around dwell, repeat risk, and residual exposure the customer can act on.
5. Communicate Residual Risk Leave a clear picture of what the platform addresses, what remains open, and what to watch.

In a customer conversation the sequence confirms the reported condition, validates it with evidence, right-sizes the next step in the evaluation, and leaves a residual-risk statement the customer can use. Case studies below are supporting evidence of that evaluation process under incomplete data.

How I Run a First Call

Open — Confirm the condition they are seeing and the window they have to act.
Discover — Separate reported symptoms from what the system and evidence actually show.
Qualify — Determine whether the platform addresses the decision point or still requires validation.
Bridge — Right-size the next step in the evaluation and quantify residual exposure.
Close — Leave a clear picture of what is addressed, what remains open, and what to watch.
